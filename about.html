The habit we're breaking

Most of us learned math by watching someone show the steps, then copying them. So that's how we teach: I do, we do, you do. Students get very good at following along, and the understanding never gets built.

That's math mimicking. It isn't a teacher problem. It's a habit almost all of us inherited, and habits can change.

Stop Math Mimicking helps K–12 math teachers make that change, so their students reason, explain, and make sense of math instead of copying it.

What we believe

  • Name the habit, not the teacher. We were all taught to mimic. Teachers are the solution, not the problem.
  • Sense-making over steps. Students who understand why remember how.
  • Ready for tomorrow. Every idea comes with a move a teacher can try in the next lesson.
  • Classroom-tested, research-grounded. Built in real classrooms and rooted in math education research.
  • From one classroom to a whole district. The same approach works for a single teacher or a system.

Amanda is a math educator who has worked as a classroom teacher and in math coaching and leadership roles, and she holds a doctorate in education. She speaks and writes about math teaching and learning.
